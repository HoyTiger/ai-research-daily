This effort has produced agentic frameworks that orchestrate perception, memory, and decision-making around powerful LLM backbones. With the advent of large multimodal models (LMMs), these systems can process and integrate diverse modalities, including images, audio, and video, thereby improving their real-world applicability. Yet, while surveys of LLM-based agents exist, the role of multimodality in shaping agency has not been systematically examined in recent years. This survey fills the gap by analyzing the impact of multimodality across the core functional modules of the agentic framework: perception, reasoning, planning, memory, and action. Using this lens, we trace the evolution from text-centric agents to multimodal frameworks, examine how modalities are integrated through delegated, late-fusion, and early-fusion architectures, and assess the emergence of agentic behaviors enabled by grounded perception and multimodal reasoning. Beyond capabilities, we analyze performance across these settings and discuss efficiency-scalability trade-offs, including training and inference costs, latency, and deployment constraints. By focusing on the impact of multimodality in agentic design, we aim to identify key gaps and chart a roadmap toward robust and general-purpose intelligent systems.</p></details>

  <details><summary>查看原始摘要</summary><p>Decentralized multi-agent systems built on large language models distribute capabilities across agents that collaborate through local handoffs. We show that this collaboration can carry an attacker-controlled task beyond a compromised agent’s own capabilities, without the attacker knowing the target’s location or planning the subsequent route. We term this process a Self-Routing Delegation Attack (SRDA): downstream agents’ ordinary collaboration decisions carry the task toward a target capability. We model SRDA as a finite-horizon stochastic process to analyze how agent decisions and collaboration topology shape attack success. We examine two mechanisms: handoff guidance that encourages agents to continue delegating, and downstream feedback that enables further decisions after collaborators respond. Through a recursion for attack success probability, we derive sufficient conditions under which each mechanism increases attack success and their combination produces gains beyond the sum of their separate effects. Controlled experiments across six model configurations and 14 collaboration topologies show that delegation guidance and natural-language feedback jointly raise attack success by up to 26.24 percentage points over the baseline without either addition. In the tested topologies, deeper chains exhibit larger guidance gains, while greater branching strengthens the interaction between guidance and feedback. These findings show that restricting an individual agent’s capabilities does not necessarily contain the consequences of its compromise. Security evaluation must account for capabilities reachable through collaboration and whether task authorization is maintained across handoffs and recovery.   <details><summary>查看原始摘要</summary><p>How multimodal language models should encode visual input remains up for debate. Most models use a pretrained vision encoder, while some recent recipes train the encoder from scratch with the language model or remove it entirely. Vision encoders bundle together three design choices: a visual prior from upstream pretraining, dedicated vision capacity, and pre-fusion placement of that capacity (i.e., before the multimodal backbone). Because published models also differ in data, architecture, and compute, comparing them cannot isolate the effect of each choice. We disentangle these choices via a controlled framework where we pretrain all models from scratch on common data and compare them on visual question answering and language benchmarks at matched lifetime compute. We find that the visual prior is not necessary; an encoder trained jointly from scratch is on par with a pretrained one, without a separate training stage. What matters instead is dedicated vision capacity: an encoder-free model trails on visual understanding even when its backbone is enlarged by the encoder&#x27;s parameter count. Yet that capacity need not precede multimodal fusion, as vision-only modules added in-fusion to early backbone layers perform comparably to a pre-fusion encoder and can lower time to first token. These results also hold across backbone sizes, when encoder pretraining is treated as free, and when starting from a pretrained language model. Overall, our findings favor dedicated vision capacity trained jointly with the backbone and placed early within it.</p></details>
