  <h3><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10439" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">谁记得什么? 用于多用途、多剂相互作用的多角内存</a></h3>
  <p class="paper-original-title">Who Remembers What? Multi-Granularity Memory Extraction for Multi-User, Multi-Agent Interaction</p>
  <p class="paper-authors">Yunzhe Wang、Mesut Erhan Unal、Sheng Zhang、Shabnam Ghadar、Peng Tang</p>
  <p class="paper-summary">自我演化的智能体商通过在智能体商之间分享这些教训,提高自身的技能,记忆力,利用经验的代码而无需再训练基础LLM,团体演化的智能体商更进一步. 然而,大多数这类系统忽略了,用户和不同行为者之间的分享需要决定谁能从每一教训中受益,哪些信息必须保持保密。 我们提出MixUA,这是一个内存系统,将独立的用户-智能体轨迹提炼成课程,并将每个课程分配给用户-智能体对子,用户,智能体,或全球范围,它根据谁受益以及它可能披露的内容. 为了评估,我们引入了 CrossLearn Bench, 一个混合用户-智能体基准,该基准将既定的智能体任务与用户偏好和隐私探针(tes)对齐</p>
  
  <div class="paper-tags"><span>大模型</span><span>Agent</span><span>评测与安全</span><span>community pick</span></div>
  <details><summary>查看原始摘要</summary><p>Self-evolving agents improve their own skills, memory, and harness code from experience without retraining the base LLM, and group-evolving agents go further by sharing these lessons across agents. Yet most such systems overlook that sharing across users and heterogeneous agents requires deciding who can benefit from each lesson and what information must remain private. We present MixUA, a memory system that distills independent user-agent trajectories into lessons and assigns each lesson to a user-agent pair, user, agent, or global scope according to whom it benefits and what it may disclose. For evaluation, we introduce CrossLearnBench, a mixed-user-agent benchmark that pairs established agent tasks with user preferences and privacy probes that test whether private facts leak into memory other users can read. With five users and seven agents, MixUA raises pass rates by up to 16 points over no memory and 5 over a single global memory, even on tasks previously tried only by other users on other agents. It retains up to 90% of previously stated preferences, and at most 0.02% of privacy probes find a planted private fact in a forbidden scope. We hope MixUA offers a scalable path toward a global system where users and agents co-evolve.</p></details>

  <p class="paper-original-title">TurtLES: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Turbulent 3D Neural PDE Surrogates</p>
  <p class="paper-authors">Jarno Platenburg、Armand Kassaï Koupaï、Paola Cinnella、Patrick Gallinari</p>
  <p class="paper-summary">机器学习代用品越来越多地用于加速计算流体动态,但由于缺乏掌握现实的、依赖时间的动荡流动的基准,进展受到限制。 我们引入了TurtLES, 一个由剪切层过渡系统中的 3D 波动圆柱组成的14个TB数据集, 通过高纯度隐含的大型编辑模拟计算。 数据集包含400个轨迹,每个时速400步,每帧有3-1100万点,跨越几何,雷诺兹数,攻击角度的变化. 与现有的数据集不同的是,图尔特莱斯结合了(一)物理上现实的动荡与由涡旋拉伸驱动的主动能量级联,(二)密集的不规则网格,(三)长视时间动态. 我们的基准状态</p>
  
  <div class="paper-tags"><span>生成与创造</span><span>评测与安全</span><span>community pick</span></div>
  <details><summary>查看原始摘要</summary><p>Machine-learning surrogates are increasingly used to accelerate computational fluid dynamics, yet progress is limited by the lack of benchmarks capturing realistic, time-dependent turbulent flows. We introduce TurtLES, a 14 TB dataset of 3D turbulent cylinder wakes in the shear-layer transition regime, computed via high-fidelity implicit large-eddy simulations. The dataset contains 400 trajectories of 400 time steps each, with 3-11 million points per frame, spanning variations in geometry, Reynolds number, and angle of attack. Unlike existing datasets, TurtLES combines (i) physically realistic turbulence with an active energy cascade driven by vortex stretching, (ii) dense irregular meshes, and (iii) long-horizon temporal dynamics. We benchmark state-of-the-art neural operators on autoregressive rollouts and identify key failure modes, including temporal instability, loss of small-scale energy, and challenges in learning on large irregular domains. TurtLES provides a new benchmark for evaluating surrogate models on 3D turbulent wakes at industrially relevant mesh resolution.</p></details>
